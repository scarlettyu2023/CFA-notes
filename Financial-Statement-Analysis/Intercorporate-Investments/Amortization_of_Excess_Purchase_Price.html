CFA Level II · Financial Statement Analysis · Module 1.05

Excess Purchase Price,
Fair Value Option & Impairment

How the premium paid above book value is decomposed, amortized, and tested for impairment under IFRS and US GAAP.

01 Why purchase price usually exceeds book value
Assets at historical cost
Book value reflects what the investee originally paid for assets — not today's market value. Especially true for PP&E and intangibles.
Unrecorded intangibles
Brands, customer relationships, patents, and proprietary technology often have zero book value but substantial real worth.
Goodwill and synergies
The buyer may pay a premium for expected synergies, market position, or future earnings potential that isn't on any balance sheet.
IFRS vs US GAAP: PP&E measurement
IFRS allows companies to carry PP&E at either historical cost or fair value (revaluation model). US GAAP requires historical cost only. This means the gap between purchase price and book value is often larger for US GAAP investees.
02 Decomposing the excess purchase price
Purchase price paid
Total cost to acquire the stake
Investor's share of book value
Ownership % × investee's net assets at book value
↓
Excess purchase price
Purchase price minus investor's share of book value. This total gets split into two buckets below.
↙
↘
FV adjustments to identifiable assets
Allocated to specific assets (PP&E, inventory, intangibles, land) based on fair value minus book value × ownership %. Amortized over useful life.
Goodwill — residual
Whatever remains after allocating to all identifiable assets. Not amortized. Tested for impairment. Included in carrying value of the investment (not a separate line).
↓                                ↓
Amortized over useful life
Reduces equity income and carrying value each period. Land: indefinite → no amortization. Inventory: expensed when sold.
Not amortized — indefinite life
Reviewed for impairment. Written down if impaired. Impairment loss on income statement; carrying value reduced.
03 How amortization flows through the investment account
= Starting investment balance (cost) recorded at acquisition
+ Investor's share of investee net income equity income line
− Dividends received return of capital, not income
− Amortization of FV premium investor records privately; not on investee's books
= Ending carrying value of investment
Alternative view of the carrying value at any point in time
Carrying value = (Ownership % × investee's net assets at book value) + unamortized excess purchase price
As years pass and FV premiums amortize, the carrying value converges toward the pure book value proportion. Goodwill stays in the unamortized balance indefinitely (until impaired).

04 Fair value option
Irrevocable election
Must be chosen at initial recognition. Cannot switch back to the equity method later.
Unrealized gains and losses
Changes in fair value plus interest and dividends all flow through the income statement each period.
No amortization, no goodwill
The entire complexity of excess purchase price allocation disappears. Investment is simply marked to market.
IFRS
Available only to:
· Venture capital organizations
· Mutual funds
· Unit trusts
· Investment-linked insurance funds

Heavily restricted
US GAAP
Available to:
· All entities
· No restrictions on entity type


Available to everyone
05 Impairment of equity method investments
Trigger (both IFRS and US GAAP)
Fair value of the investment falls below its carrying value and the decline is considered other than temporary (US GAAP) or is supported by objective evidence of a loss event (IFRS).
↓
IFRS — recoverable amount test
Compare carrying value to recoverable amount
(higher of value in use or fair value less costs to sell)

Impairment loss → income statement
Carrying value reduced directly or via allowance

Goodwill tested as part of total investment — not separately

Reversal permitted ↑ allowed if recoverable amount later increases
US GAAP — permanent decline test
If decline is determined to be permanent:
impairment loss recognized on income statement
Carrying value written down to fair value

No separate goodwill test — whole investment tested


Reversal prohibited ↓ not allowed even if fair value recovers
Key exam distinction — impairment reversal
IFRS permits reversal of a prior impairment if the investment's recoverable amount subsequently increases — the carrying value is written back up, capped at what it would have been had no impairment been recognized.
US GAAP prohibits any reversal. Once impaired, the new lower carrying value becomes the new cost basis. This is a reliable exam differentiator.

07 Decision tree — handling excess purchase price
Did investor pay more than their share of the investee's book value?
↳ Yes ↓
Can the excess be attributed to specific identifiable assets?
↳ Yes → Allocate to those assets (PP&E, intangibles, inventory) amortize over useful life
↳ Remainder → goodwill — not amortized
Did investor elect the fair value option at inception?
↳ Yes → No allocation, no amortization, no goodwill mark to market each period
↳ No → Use equity method with amortization adjustments
Has investment value declined below carrying value?
↳ IFRS: Test recoverable amount → recognize loss → reversal allowed
↳ GAAP: If permanent decline → recognize loss → no reversal
