2. Financial asset categories (when ownership < 20%)

FVTPL
Fair value through profit & loss. Gains/losses → income statement. Default category.
FVOCI
Fair value through OCI. Gains/losses → equity only. Irrevocable election for equity instruments.
Amortized cost
Debt only. Held to collect contractual cash flows. No mark-to-market.
IFRS 9 uses a two-part test to assign category
① Business model test — is the asset held to collect, to sell, or both?  ·  ② Cash flow characteristics test — are CFs solely payments of principal and interest?  ·  US GAAP (ASC 320/321) uses similar but differently named categories.

3. Equity method mechanics

Balance sheet — 1 line
"Investment in associate"
= carrying value of net interest
Income statement — 1 line
"Equity income in associate"
= your % share of net profit
How the carrying value moves each period
+ Initial cost at acquisition
+ Your share of investee net income
− Your share of investee net losses
− Dividends received (return of capital, NOT income)
± Your share of investee OCI items
= Ending carrying value
Two special rules to remember
If value reaches zero: stop recording losses. Resume only after unrecognized losses are fully recovered from future profits.
Dividends: reduce carrying value — they are NOT recognized as income.

4. Equity method vs proportionate consolidation (joint ventures)

Equity method (required)
B/S: one line — "investment in JV"
I/S: one line — "equity income in JV"
Total assets: lower
Total debt: lower
Revenue: lower
Net income: same as prop. consolidation
Net assets: same as prop. consolidation
Proportionate consolidation (rare exception)
B/S: your % of each asset and liability
I/S: your % of each revenue and expense
Total assets: higher
Total debt: higher
Revenue: higher
Net income: same as equity method
Net assets: same as equity method
Key insight for the exam
Net income and net assets are identical under both methods. The difference is in gross presentation — which changes leverage, asset turnover, and margin ratios even though the underlying economics are the same.

5. Business combinations — full consolidation

Acquisition method
Required under both IFRS 3 and ASC 805. Assets and liabilities recorded at fair value on acquisition date.
Goodwill
Purchase price minus fair value of net assets acquired. Tested for impairment annually — never amortized.
Non-controlling interest (NCI)
The portion of subsidiary equity not owned by parent. Shown separately in consolidated equity.
IFRS vs GAAP difference on goodwill impairment
Both test for impairment rather than amortizing. GAAP uses a single-step test; IFRS uses a recoverable amount test. Both are broadly aligned — the frameworks converged here.

6. Special purpose entities and variable interest entities

The consolidation trigger
Not ownership % — it's economic risk and reward
If you are the primary beneficiary → consolidate
Regardless of how small your equity stake is
Common uses
Securitization of mortgages or receivables
Off-balance-sheet financing structures
Project finance and leasing vehicles

7. IFRS vs US GAAP — side by side

Financial assets
IFRS 9 vs ASC 320/321. Similar in spirit, different category names and classification tests.
Associates and JVs
Broadly aligned. Both require equity method. IAS 28 (IFRS) vs ASC Topic 323 (GAAP).
Business combinations
Largely aligned since 2008 convergence. Goodwill impairment approach differs slightly.

9. Decision tree — which method applies?

Is there control? (ownership >50% or de facto)
↳ Yes →Full consolidation (acquisition method)
↳ No ↓
Is it an SPE/VIE and are you the primary beneficiary?
↳ Yes →Full consolidation
↳ No ↓
Is there joint control with other parties?
↳ Yes →Joint venture → equity method
↳ No ↓
Is there significant influence? (~20–50%, or demonstrated)
↳ Yes →Associate → equity method
↳ No ↓
What is the investment type?
Equity instrument →FVTPL or FVOCI election
Debt, held to sell →FVTPL
Debt, held to collect + sell →FVOCI
Debt, held to collect only →Amortized cost
